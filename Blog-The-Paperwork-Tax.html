The paperwork tax: where commercial agencies actually lose the week

Ask an agency principal what's hard right now and you'll hear the same three things: experienced account managers are retiring faster than they can be replaced, the service backlog never shrinks, and renewals keep arriving sooner than anyone's ready for. Underneath all three is the same problem — most of the work isn't insurance. It's paperwork about insurance. Here are the five places we see it compound, and what we're doing about each one.

01

The re-keying tax

The same client gets typed into the AMS, then into four carrier portals, then into a supplemental application, then into a certificate form. The industry has had ACORD standards for decades; the data still moves by hand. Every re-key is minutes lost and a chance to introduce the typo that becomes an endorsement request in month three.

What we built: the fill engine. Data is extracted once — from an expiring dec, a loss run, an email thread — into the client record, and every application, portal form, and certificate fills from that single source. Every field links back to where it came from.

02

The document chase

A submission is only as fast as its slowest attachment. SOVs, driver lists, payroll schedules, signed apps — each one waits on a client who has a business to run. So mornings go to follow-up emails, and the marketing clock doesn't start until the folder is complete.

What we built: delegated document collection. BrokerOS drafts the request, follows up by email and phone until the documents arrive, files each one against the right client and policy, and tells you when the folder is ready to market.

03

The renewal crunch

Renewals surface when the expiration report says so, and the list gets triaged by memory: the loud accounts get remarketed, the quiet ones get rolled over. Late remarketing means rate surprises for the client — and a lapsed-coverage E&O claim is the kind that names the agency, not the carrier.

What we built: renewals that assemble themselves. The file opens months out with expiring terms, updated exposures, and loss runs already requested — so the renewal conversation starts with the client, not with the paperwork.

04

Death by certificate

No single task interrupts an account manager more than certificate requests. Each COI is five minutes of lookup, generation, and logging — times thirty a day in a construction-heavy book, arriving as phone calls in the middle of everything else.

What we built: the AI voice agent handles COI requests end to end — verifies the caller, adds the holder, issues the certificate, logs it on the policy — and flags anything outside its authority to a human instead of guessing.

05

The unlogged file

Every E&O defense comes down to documentation, and the most dangerous work in an agency is the work that never made it into the file: the coverage question answered on a cell phone, the "go ahead and bind it" voicemail, the promise made at renewal. When staff are stretched, logging is the first thing that slips.

What we built: a system where logging isn't a separate step. Calls, emails, and AI actions land on the client record with transcripts and timestamps, automatically — the audit trail is the default, not the discipline.

THE POINT

None of this asks your staff to work harder, and none of it replaces them — the fix is a management system where the paperwork does itself and people do insurance. That's the whole idea behind BrokerOS.
